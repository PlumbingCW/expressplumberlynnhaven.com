Leaking Outdoor Faucet Handle Repair in Lynn Haven, FL

If water creeps out around the handle while the spigot is on, the cause is almost always a worn seal behind it. Many fixes take one wrench and ten minutes.

Find out where the water is actually coming from

An outdoor faucet can leak in three places, and each one has a different cure. Turn the handle on and off while you watch. Water at the spout points to a worn washer. Water behind the handle points to the packing. Water at the wall points to the pipe or the joint inside.

This guide covers the middle case, a leak around the handle stem. It is the most common one, and the one most owners can handle.

The ten-minute packing nut fix

Before you start, shut off the faucet and make sure no water is running. If your faucet has a separate shutoff valve inside the home, close that too. You need an adjustable wrench and, for some models, a small screwdriver.

  1. Remove the handle

    Unscrew the center screw, if there is one, and lift the handle off the stem.

  2. Find the packing nut

    It is the hex nut just under where the handle sat. Water leaking out of the thread means the seal is loose.

  3. Tighten gently

    Turn the nut clockwise a quarter turn, then test. Overtightening makes the handle hard to turn and can crack the body.

  4. Replace the packing if it still leaks

    Unscrew the nut and replace the rubber washer or the graphite string packing, then reseat the nut.

  5. Put it back and test

    Reinstall the handle and turn the water on slowly. Look for drips under the handle and at the wall.

When a simple fix will not hold

Corrosion is the usual reason. A stem that has pitted or a body that is cracked will keep leaking no matter how tight the nut gets. Replacement parts for old faucets are sometimes hard to find, and a new stem can cost nearly as much as a new spigot.

Look at the faucet type, too. A frost-free faucet has a long stem that runs through the wall. A leak inside the wall from that style can soak sheathing and framing before you see it outside. If the handle leaks and the hose connection is dripping too, replacing the whole unit is usually cleaner.

Vacuum breakers and hose habits

Many modern outdoor faucets include a vacuum breaker, a small cap that stops dirty water from flowing back into the house supply. It may spit a little water from the cap while the faucet runs. That is normal. A steady stream with the water off is not.

Leave hoses attached to the spigot only when you need them. A hose left connected holds pressure on the seal and can keep a vacuum breaker from draining. Disconnect it when the job is done, and the faucet lasts longer.

Checklist before you call

  • The leak appears only when the handle is turned on.
  • You tightened the packing nut a quarter turn and tested again.
  • The stem is not corroded and the handle turns smoothly.
  • There is no wetness inside the wall or the cabinet on the other side.
  • The faucet is not frost-free with a leak inside the wall.

If any of the last two items fail, shut off the water to the faucet and call. A plumber can swap a stem, replace the faucet or repair the supply behind it. A faucet repair visit covers most spigot problems, and leak detection helps if water is appearing somewhere you cannot see.

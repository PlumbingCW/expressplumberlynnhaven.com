Where the city's job ends and yours begins

Springfield was incorporated on February 12, 1935, and it operates its own public works department, with on-call lines for water and sewer. That matters on a bad night. A main break in the street, a dry tap for the whole block or a manhole overflowing belongs to the city. Call its on-call line for those.

Everything after the meter is the homeowner's. That includes the service line running to the house, the shutoff valves, the water heater and every drain inside. If the meter reads moving while all fixtures are off, the loss is on your side, and a leak detection visit with an acoustic microphone finds it without digging blind.

On the sewer side, the same split applies. The city runs the main in the street. Where the city's responsibility ends and yours begins varies, so ask the city before you assume. A backup that only affects your home points to the lateral.

After a boil water notice, check your plumbing

On May 1, 2025, Springfield issued a precautionary boil water notice. It covered homes between 3rd Street and Bayou Drive and between Everitt Avenue and Bob Little Road. The reports did not give a cause. Notices like this are common after pressure drops, and the plumbing in the house can matter afterward.

While a notice is active, boil drinking water for at least one minute, or use bottled water. Do not trust the ice maker or the filter pitcher. After the city lifts the notice, run the cold taps for a few minutes and flush the ice maker and water dispenser lines.

Replace the filter cartridges that were in use during the notice. If the water heater sat unused or the water runs discolored for more than a few minutes, ask a plumber to check the tank and sediment.

Backflow devices and why the city keeps a program

Springfield's public works department has a backflow prevention device program page. A backflow preventer stops water from flowing backward from a lawn sprinkler, a boiler or a hose into the drinking supply. It is a small valve doing a large job.

If you have an irrigation system, a fire line or a commercial kitchen, check the city page for what applies to your property. A device that leaks from its relief port or has not been tested is worth a call. For business owners on Transmitter Road, commercial plumbing crews work with these devices regularly.

Code and backflow standards

When a commercial plumbing emergency cannot wait for Monday

A commercial plumbing emergency in Lynn Haven, FL means water on the floor, a closed dining room, or restrooms the public cannot use, and our 24/7 line is staffed for it. While you wait, close the nearest fixture valve or the building's main, stop using connected drains, and keep staff and customers off wet electrical areas.

Tell the dispatcher what kind of space it is, whether it is a tenant space or the whole building, and whether you can reach the shutoff. Property managers calling for tenants can say so up front, and the plumber will arrange access with the site contact.

Grease, floor drains, and why kitchens back up

Fats, oils and grease cool and solidify in the trap and the line, and a restaurant produces far more of it than a home does. The usual signs are a floor drain that drains slowly at the end of service, odor near the dish pit, and a three-compartment sink that stands full.

A plumber can cable the line, hydro jet it, and inspect it with a camera, and the right tool depends on whether the blockage is soft grease, scale, or a collapsed section. For repeat trouble, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall instead of just boring a hole through the clog.

A grease trap or interceptor must be pumped on a schedule set by the local rules and your own volume. Ask the city utility or the building department what applies to your space.

Leak detection in a commercial building

A hidden leak in a shop or office shows up as a water bill that climbs, a damp wall, or a meter that moves with everything shut off. Finding it without opening every wall means using pressure testing, an acoustic ground microphone on slab runs, and a thermal camera for heated lines.

Commercial leaks hurt when they sit above a drop ceiling, behind a storefront wall, or under a slab. Our leak detection page lists the methods in detail, and the same approach applies to commercial supply lines.

Mention whether the building shares a meter with other tenants, because that changes how the plumber isolates one space.

Backflow and cross-connection protection

Backflow devices keep dirty water from being pulled back into the potable supply. Restaurants, car washes, irrigation systems and some fire lines commonly need an assembly installed, and many require periodic testing.

Requirements depend on the water provider and on what is connected to the line, so ask the City of Lynn Haven's public utilities office or your water provider which rules cover your space. A plumber can then install, repair or replace the device and coordinate the test.

What affects the cost of commercial plumbing

  • Emergency visit at night or on a weekend versus a scheduled day.
  • Pipe size and access, including ceiling height and slab cuts.
  • The tool required, such as cabling, jetting or a camera.
  • Code items found on site that must be corrected.
  • Parts and fixtures specified by the building.

Commercial property insurance may respond to sudden, accidental water damage but not to ongoing seepage or poor maintenance, so check your policy. For your real number, call the 24/7 line. A residential sewer problem at a home is covered on sewer line repair.

Codes that shape commercial work

Commercial buildings follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which calls for grease interceptors on many food service drains and for backflow protection where a contamination risk exists. The plumber and your city building department decide what your building needs.

What to do before the plumber arrives at a business

  • Close the main water valve or the valve to the affected fixture.
  • Keep customers and staff away from standing water and any sewage.
  • Switch off equipment that sits in or near water, such as dishwashers and ice machines, if you can do it from a dry spot.
  • Take photos of the damage and the source for your insurer.
  • If you smell gas anywhere in the building, leave and call 911 or the gas utility first.

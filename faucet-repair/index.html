Shutoff valves that fail when you need them

Many complaints start when the valve that should stop the water does not. Angle stops under a sink sit unmoved for years, and the internal washer or stem can seize or weep once you finally turn them.

If a stop valve leaks, the plumber may shut the main, replace the valve, and test the line before leaving. Good practice is to exercise every fixture stop once a year so you know which ones work.

On an older home with galvanized supply lines, a worn valve can sometimes signal wider corrosion. In that case, ask whether repiping is worth discussing.

Repair or replace the faucet

Many faucets are worth repairing, particularly when the body is sound and only a cartridge has worn out. Replacement makes more sense in the cases below.

Corroded body

Green or white crust around the base, or a body that leaks from the casting, cannot be sealed by new parts.

Parts no longer sold

Older single-brand models can be harder to source than a new faucet is to install.

Low flow

Mineral buildup inside the spout can restrict flow even after cleaning the aerator.

Finish wear

Peeling plating shows the base metal is going, and leaks often follow.

How the repair visit runs

  1. Call and describe

    Tell the dispatcher the fixture, the brand if you know it, and how the water behaves.

  2. Shutoff and diagnosis

    The plumber closes the line, removes the handle and inspects the cartridge, seats and supply.

  3. Repair or swap

    Parts are replaced, or a new faucet is mounted with fresh supply lines and a tight drain connection.

  4. Test under pressure

    Water is turned back on and the base, handles and lines are checked dry with a paper towel.

What drives the cost of faucet repair

  • Cartridge or trim kit repair versus a full faucet replacement.
  • Whether the shutoff valves under the sink must be replaced too.
  • Sink type: drop-in, undermount, or a vessel sink with a tall faucet.
  • Hidden damage to the cabinet floor or drain.
  • Time of day, since nights and weekends carry a premium.

Homeowners insurance often responds to sudden, accidental water release but not slow, long-term seepage, so check your policy before assuming. For your real number, call the 24/7 line and describe the fixture. A cracked trap under the same sink is covered on the drain cleaning page.

Standards for replacement parts

Faucets and fittings sold for drinking water use must meet lead-free requirements under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act, and faucet performance is tested to standards such as ASME A112.18.1. A plumber can match a cartridge to your model and confirm the part is rated for potable water.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Close the shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise.
  • Put a bucket under the trap and dry the cabinet floor.
  • Do not force a seized handle, because a cracked body means a full replacement.
  • Take a photo of the brand name and any model number on the faucet.

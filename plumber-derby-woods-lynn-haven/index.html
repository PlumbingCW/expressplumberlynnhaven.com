Slab homes and hidden leaks

Many Gulf Coast homes sit on concrete slab foundations, and the home above is one of them. When the water lines run in or under a slab, a leak has no wall to show it. The signs are quieter.

  • A warm or hot spot on a floor with no heater under it.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • A water bill that climbs with no change in habits.
  • A meter that keeps turning with all taps closed.
  • Damp carpet or lifting flooring along one wall.

Slab leak repair starts by confirming the leak with listening gear. Then the plumber offers choices such as spot repair or rerouting the line.

Drains in a single-story house

A single-story house has one level of drains, and they all run to the same main line. When a tub and a toilet slow down together, the problem is usually the main, not each fixture.

Try this first. Run water in the fixture farthest from the main and watch the nearest one. If water rises there, call for drain cleaning or a sewer camera inspection before the line backs up.

Before a replacement or remodel

For a water heater swap, a repipe or a bath remodel, permit rules vary, so ask the Lynn Haven building department what your job needs. Ask who pulls the permit. A water heater replacement usually takes a few hours once the unit is on site, and the plumber will say what your house needs. The work falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, so a permitted install is inspected to that code. MyFloridaLicense.com lets you check a plumber first.

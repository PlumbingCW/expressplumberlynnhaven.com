When a hidden water leak is an emergency: what to do while you wait

A leak you cannot see is still an emergency, because water keeps working on wood, drywall and slab around the clock. Our 24/7 line is staffed for exactly this call, and you can protect the house while a plumber is on the way. Emergency leak detection in Lynn Haven, FL starts with a short phone conversation about what you have noticed.

Do these steps first. They cost nothing and they limit the damage.

  • Look at the water meter with every fixture off. A spinning dial or a moving leak indicator means water is escaping somewhere.
  • Close the main shutoff valve at the house if you see water pooling or a stain spreading.
  • Switch off power to any room with wet walls, ceilings or outlets. If the breaker panel is in a wet area, stay out and call.
  • Take dated photos and short videos of every stain, drip and meter reading for your insurance claim.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the wet zone.

Water meter test: is the leak inside or outside the house?

This quick test tells the plumber which half of the system to search first. Many callers tell us the meter keeps running with everything off, and this is the way to confirm it.

  1. Stop all water use

    Turn off taps, the ice maker, the dishwasher, the washing machine and any irrigation. Make sure nobody flushes.

  2. Read the meter

    Lift the meter box lid and find the small triangle or dial that spins with any flow. Note the numbers on the register.

  3. Wait and recheck

    Wait 15 to 30 minutes, then look again. Any movement means a leak exists.

  4. Close the house main

    Shut the main valve at the house and watch the meter. If it stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the water service line between the meter and the house.

High water bill and no wet spot to show for it

A bill that jumps with no change in habits is the most common reason people call. The city of Lynn Haven runs its own water system, and its utility bill is the first place a hidden leak shows up. The cause is often small and fixable once someone finds it. Properties on larger lots, such as those on Northshore Circle, give a leak more ground to hide in, so the Northshore page lists meter checks for big yards.

Silent toilet leak

A worn flapper lets water slip into the bowl all day. A few drops of food coloring in the tank will show it within minutes.

Irrigation or hose bib

A stuck sprinkler valve or a cracked outdoor spigot can run for weeks without anyone noticing.

Water heater relief valve

A weeping temperature and pressure relief valve drips to a drain line you may never see.

Service line break

A crack in the buried pipe from the meter to the house wastes water that never shows up inside.

Compare your recent bills, then call with the numbers. A weeping valve on the water heater is a very different job from a buried line.

How plumbers find leaks without tearing out walls

Pinpointing comes before cutting. Each method answers a different question, and a good visit often combines two or three.

Ceilings, walls, crawlspaces and attic leaks

A brown ring on a ceiling rarely sits under the pipe that caused it. Water follows framing and drywall before it shows itself, so the stain can be several feet from the source. A moisture meter and thermal camera trace it back through the bathroom above and the supply lines in between.

Raised homes and crawlspaces show sagging insulation, damp soil and a musty smell. A warm patch on a slab floor is a different problem, covered under slab leak repair.

Yard, irrigation and buried water line leaks

Soggy grass, a low spot that never dries or a sudden drop in water pressure can mean the water service line is leaking underground. Meter box leaks at the connection can look the same. The plumber confirms with the meter test, then listens along the line with ground microphones before anyone digs.

Call 811 before any digging so buried utilities are marked. A fully broken service line is a job for burst pipe repair. Permit rules vary, so ask the Lynn Haven building department.

What changes the price of leak detection

Every visit is different, so we do not quote from a script. These are the factors that move the number.

  • How many methods the search needs before the leak is pinned down.
  • Whether the leak is accessible, behind tile, under slab or buried in the yard.
  • How much of the home's plumbing has to be isolated and pressure tested.
  • The pipe material and age, and whether a repair or a section replacement follows.
  • After-hours timing and the access needed for the repair itself.

Pressure and code

Water pressure above about 80 psi stresses pipes and fittings, and the International Plumbing Code (IPC) sets a limit on static pressure in building supply systems. A pressure test is often part of finding a leak, and the plumber may suggest a pressure reducing valve if yours is high.

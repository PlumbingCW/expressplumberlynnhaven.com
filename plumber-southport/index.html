Crossing North Bay to reach Southport

The Bailey Bridge, built in 1946 to replace a wooden pier, carries State Road 77 across North Bay between Lynn Haven and Southport. Dispatch from the Highway 77 office is about 8 miles. Southport's own road network runs along Edwards Road, County Road 388, State Road 20 and County Road 2321.

Name the nearest of those roads when you call. In a spread-out community, the road name gets a plumber to the right turn faster than a street number alone.

Septic or sewer: know which one your drain feeds

About 800 homes near Deer Point Lake are on septic systems. Bay County's North Bay Water Quality Improvement Program lets existing homes switch from septic to sewer, and the switch is voluntary with a cost that depends on distance to the nearest connection. Hodges Bayou Plantation, a newer Southport development, is sewer-only.

That mix means two neighbors can have very different drain problems. A septic home with slow drains might have a full tank, a clogged baffle or a saturated drain field. A sewer home with slow drains has a clog in the lateral or the main.

Do not pour chemical cleaners into a septic system, because they upset the bacteria that break down waste. Mention your system type on the call, and the dispatcher can confirm what equipment suits the job.

New homes and fast growth since Hurricane Michael

Southport has grown quickly since Hurricane Michael, and new construction brings its own plumbing calls. Modern homes are usually plumbed in PEX with push or crimp fittings. A loose fitting or a nicked line shows up as a damp wall or a water bill that creeps upward.

If your house is new, check whether the builder's warranty still covers the plumbing before you pay for repairs. The plumber can document what failed, which helps with any claim.

A leak detection visit confirms whether the loss is in the slab, the wall or the yard line. Many homes in this part of the Gulf Coast sit on slabs, so a hidden leak can run for weeks before it shows.

Septic and sewer code questions

Whether a home uses septic or sewer, plumbing inside the house follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Switching from septic to sewer is a separate program, so ask the county about its North Bay Water Quality Improvement Program.

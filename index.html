Emergency plumbing when the water will not wait

Lynn Haven Express Plumbing answers one phone line around the clock. When you call about a burst pipe, a sewage backup or a flooding water heater, the dispatcher takes your address, asks a few questions about what is leaking and where, and sends a licensed plumber.

Most callers have the same first question: what do I do right now? Shut off the main water valve, switch off power to any wet room, and move anything valuable out of the water's path. Those three steps limit damage while the plumber drives over.

Not every call is an emergency, and that is fine. A dripping faucet, a slow drain or a water heater that needs replacing can be booked for the next available appointment, and the dispatcher will say when that is.

  • Burst or leaking pipes, including burst pipe repair and slab leaks.
  • Clogged drains and sewer backups through drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning.
  • No hot water, a leaking tank or a failed element on a water heater.
  • Overflowing toilets, jammed disposals and fixtures that will not shut off.

What to do before the plumber arrives

A few minutes of preparation changes how the visit goes. The plumber can start work sooner when the water is already off and the work area is clear.

  1. Find the main shutoff

    It is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

  2. Cut power near water

    If water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, turn off those breakers only if the floor around the panel is dry.

  3. Clear the work area

    Move rugs, boxes and furniture away from the leak or the drain so the plumber has room.

  4. Note what you saw

    Write down when it started, which fixtures are affected and any sounds or smells. Take photos for your insurance claim.

  5. Keep gas off the table

    If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call a plumber.

Why Bay County plumbing calls follow the weather

Bay County sits on the Gulf Coast, and heavy rain is part of the plumbing picture. After a week of rain in October 2021, the Florida DEP reported a spill of about 500,000 gallons of raw sewage at 12th Street and Virginia Avenue in Lynn Haven. The city's utility director said stormwater was getting into the gravity sewer collection system, as reported by WTVY.

For a homeowner the lesson is practical. When the ground is saturated, older gravity sewer lines take on extra water, and a slow drain or a gurgling toilet can be the first sign. Call early, before sewage reaches the floor, and read our guide to sewer line repair if you suspect a damaged lateral.

Hurricane Michael came ashore on the Bay County coast on October 10, 2018, as a Category 5 storm. Storm damage can leave cracked supply lines and shifted pipes that do not leak for months, so a leak detection visit is worth booking after any major storm repair.

Standards behind the work

Licensed plumbers in Florida work to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Permit rules vary by city, so ask the Lynn Haven building department what your job needs. You can confirm any contractor's license on MyFloridaLicense.com before work starts.

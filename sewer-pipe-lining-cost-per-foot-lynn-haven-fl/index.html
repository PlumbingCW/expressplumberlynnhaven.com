Sewer Pipe Lining Cost Per Foot in Lynn Haven, FL

Lining repairs a cracked or root-invaded sewer pipe from the inside. Here is what drives the per-foot price and how to read a quote.

The short answer on lining prices

Lining is priced by the foot, and the range is wide. One published cost guide lists residential sewer and epoxy lining at about $80 to $250 per linear foot. Read it as typical US ranges from published cost guides, not a Lynn Haven quote. Lining often costs less overall than digging up a yard, but not in every case.

The final price depends on pipe diameter, run length, pipe condition and how hard the line is to reach. A twenty foot repair and a sixty foot full-line job are different projects. Only a camera inspection of your own pipe gives a number you can plan around.

Lining versus digging

Lining is called trenchless because the crew works from existing access points instead of opening a long trench. It suits pipes that are cracked, leaking at joints or invaded by roots but still hold their round shape. The new liner cures into a smooth pipe inside the old one.

Lining cannot fix a pipe that has collapsed, sags into a bellied low spot, or has pulled apart. Those lines need a spot dig or full replacement. A sewer line repair visit can cover either path once the camera shows what is wrong.

What a fair quote should spell out

  1. The footage of pipe being lined, with the camera video as proof of condition.
  2. How the line is cleaned first, and whether hydro jetting is included.
  3. Where access pits or cleanouts will be placed, and who restores the surface.
  4. The liner material and the cure method in plain words.
  5. A final camera pass after curing, with the video handed to you.
  6. Whether the warranty is written down and what it excludes.

Ask a plumber to explain every line of the quote. If a number cannot be tied to footage, access or materials, ask again.

Sewer facts for Lynn Haven homeowners

In October 2021 the Florida DEP released a report on a spill of about 500,000 gallons of raw sewage in Lynn Haven. It happened at 12th Street and Virginia Avenue. According to a WTVY news report, the city's utility director said stormwater was getting into the gravity sewer collection system after a week of heavy rain. He also cited damage from hurricane recovery work.

The lesson for a homeowner is practical. Cracks and open joints in sewer pipe let groundwater and roots in, and heavy rain makes that worse. A lining job seals those entry points on your side of the connection, though it cannot fix the public system. The city's Public Utilities Department page also says to call 811 before digging and to file a Water and Sewer Availability form to connect to city infrastructure.

Turning a per-foot range into a budget

Multiply the range by the footage on your camera video to see the spread. Thirty feet at $80 to $250 per foot works out to roughly $2,400 to $7,500 before access, cleaning and restoration. That is arithmetic on a published range, not a price for your home.

Longer runs pull the total up fast, and extra items such as pre-repair or a surface patch sit on top. Some jobs line only the damaged section, which keeps the footage low. Ask the plumber to price a partial section and the full run side by side so you see what each choice buys.

Where lining fits and where it does not

Lining makes the most sense when the camera shows a sound pipe with defects, such as hairline cracks, root intrusion or porous clay. It makes less sense when the pipe is broken or the slope is wrong. If the pipe is only clogged, cleaning may be enough. Hydro jetting clears grease and roots and also prepares the pipe for any lining.

Drain clog emergency: what to do while you wait

A blocked drain is an emergency when water is rising toward the rim, a toilet is backing up, or several fixtures gurgle at once. Our Lynn Haven line is open 24/7, so call at any hour. While you wait, stop using every fixture that drains into the blocked line.

Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into standing water. It burns skin on contact and makes the job riskier for the person who opens the pipe. A plain cup plunger on a sink or tub is fine, and a toilet flange plunger works for a single blocked toilet.

  • Stop dishwashers, washing machines and showers until the drain is clear.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing water that came up from a floor drain.
  • Move rugs and stored boxes off the floor near the affected drain.
  • Note which fixtures are slow, since that tells the plumber where the blockage sits.
  • Skip the chemicals and the metal coat hanger, which scratch pipe and do little.

Which drain is it? Symptoms and the usual cause

Where the water backs up usually points to where the clog lives. One slow fixture means a branch line problem, while several fixtures failing together means the main drain.

Kitchen sink clogs and what grease does to a line

Kitchen sinks account for most single-fixture clogs. Cooking fat cools inside the pipe and coats the wall, and coffee grounds and rice stick to that film. A garbage disposal grinds food finely but does not stop the buildup.

A kitchen line under a concrete slab is harder to reach than one in a crawl space. The plumber works through a cleanout or the trap with a small cable, and may suggest a jet if the grease is thick. If the disposal hums but will not drain, see garbage disposal repair first.

Snake, auger or jet: how the plumber picks a method

Hand auger.

A short hand-cranked cable for a sink, tub or toilet trap. Good for hair, soap and a dropped item.

Drum cable machine.

A motorized cable with a cutting head. It reaches 50 feet or more into a branch line and shreds roots and debris.

Hydro jet.

A hose that drives water through a nozzle and scours the pipe wall. It is the choice for grease and repeat clogs.

Sewer camera.

A small camera on a flexible rod that shows the pipe interior. It tells the plumber if the clog is debris or a broken line.

Most calls start with a cable or auger because it is quick. If the cable stops at the same spot or the clog returns, the plumber moves to a camera and a jet. A cracked pipe cannot be cleaned into health, so the visit may end with a sewer line repair recommendation.

Signs the blockage is in the main drain, not a fixture

A main drain blockage affects everything in the house, and the lowest drain overflows first. In many Gulf Coast homes on slab foundations, that is a shower or floor drain on the ground level. Rain matters here. After a week of heavy rain in October 2021, the city's utility director said stormwater was entering the gravity sewer system. A backup after a storm can start outside your property.

  • Two or more fixtures drain slowly at the same time.
  • A toilet flush makes a nearby shower or tub gurgle.
  • Water appears at a floor drain or the lowest tub.
  • The cleanout cap outside the house shows standing water.
  • The backup started during or just after heavy rain.

If the cleanout is full, the blockage is downstream of your house. A camera can show whether it is on your lateral, and the Lynn Haven Public Utilities Department can speak to the municipal side. The Historic Downtown page explains how to tell a city line from your own.

What affects the price of drain cleaning

A price depends on the job, so the dispatcher asks questions before booking. These factors move the number most:

  • Which line is blocked: a sink trap costs less labor than a main line.
  • Access: a cleanout speeds the job, while a pulled toilet or roof vent takes longer.
  • Method: a cable machine versus a hydro jet and its setup time.
  • Camera inspection if the clog returns or the cable hits an obstruction.
  • After-hours or weekend scheduling.

Homeowners insurance often covers sudden, accidental damage but not slow seepage or wear, so check your policy. For your real number, call the 24/7 line and describe the blockage.

Code points that affect drains

Drain performance depends on slope and venting, both set by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). A common rule of thumb is a quarter inch of fall per foot on smaller drain lines, which is why a sagging pipe holds water and clogs again.

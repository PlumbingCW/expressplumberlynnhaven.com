What a kitchen sink rough-in actually is

A sink rough-in is the hidden pipework set before walls close. It has three parts: a drain with a trap arm, a vent that lets the drain breathe, and two supply stubs for hot and cold water. Everything the sink, faucet, disposal and dishwasher will later connect to starts here.

Rough-in happens after framing and before drywall and cabinets. Once the wall is closed, a wrong stub position means opening it again. That is why the measurements matter more than the fixtures themselves.

The parts, the usual sizes and the common mistakes

Exact dimensions come from the sink and cabinet you buy, so this table shows what each part does and where jobs tend to go wrong. Heights and offsets always follow the manufacturer's rough-in sheet.

Island sinks and slab floors need extra planning

Many Gulf Coast homes sit on a concrete slab, which changes the plan when a sink moves. The drain for a new location may mean cutting the slab to reach the building drain. That job needs a saw cut, a trench, and a patch that is poured back to the same level.

An island sink has no wall behind it to carry a vent. Plumbers solve this with an island loop vent, which rises under the counter and turns back down, or with an air admittance valve where the local code accepts one. Ask the plumber which method they plan to use and why. The answer tells you whether they have read the rules for your jurisdiction.

Your pre-drywall checklist

Walk the kitchen with your plumber before the inspector or the drywall crew does. This is the cheapest time to catch a problem, because every item below is a ten minute fix now and a day of demolition later.

  • Drain stub centered on the sink base and matched to the cabinet drawing.
  • Vent connected and sloped so it cannot trap water.
  • Hot on the left and cold on the right at the faucet stubs.
  • Supply lines capped, pressure tested and held with no drop on the gauge.
  • Blocking installed behind the wall so stops and the disposal bracket have something solid to anchor to.
  • Dishwasher drain routed with a high loop or an air gap.
  • Photos taken of every stub and pipe run before the wall is closed.

How the work usually unfolds

  1. Confirm the fixtures

    Pick the sink, faucet, disposal and dishwasher first. Their spec sheets set every height and offset.

  2. Mark and cut

    The plumber marks the centerline, then drills studs or opens the floor for the new pipe path.

  3. Run drain and vent

    Pipe is set to slope toward the building drain, with the vent tied in above the trap arm.

  4. Run and test the supply

    Hot and cold lines are stubbed out, capped, and pressure tested before anything is hidden.

  5. Inspect, then close

    The inspector checks the work when one is required. Only then do insulation and drywall go up.

When to call a plumber instead of doing it yourself

Replacing a faucet or a trap under an existing sink is a weekend job. Moving a sink, adding an island fixture, or tying into a cast iron or old galvanized line is not. Mistakes in drains and vents show up as slow drainage, gurgling and sewer odor, and they hide inside the wall.

A plumbing installation visit covers new rough-in, and a faucet and sink repair call is the better fit if the plumbing already exists and only leaks or drips. Contractors near Springfield and Callaway can be booked through the same 24/7 line.

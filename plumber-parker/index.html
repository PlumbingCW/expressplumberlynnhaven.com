Heavy rain in a low-lying town

Parker was incorporated in 1967 and covers 2.43 square miles, about 20 percent of it water. Its elevation is around 10 feet. Low ground means rain has little room to drain away, and low-lying drains in a house are the first to complain.

A sewer backup after a downpour is the call this town should expect. In October 2021 neighboring Lynn Haven saw a sewage spill after a week of heavy rain, when stormwater got into the gravity sewer system. The same pressure on older sewers can push water up through the lowest drain in a house.

If a floor drain or shower starts bubbling during a storm, stop using water and call. Pouring more water down only adds to the backup, and a sewer line cleaning visit can clear a blockage before it floods the room.

What a flooded yard means for pipes

Standing water around the house can hide a leak in the supply line, and soaked ground makes a failing line harder to find. A meter that keeps turning with every fixture off is the clue. A burst pipe repair often starts with finding where the water is escaping.

Saltwater air near the bay speeds up corrosion on outdoor fittings, hose bibs and exposed valves. Look at them each spring. A green crust or a drip at the stem is cheap to fix now and expensive to ignore.

Ask whether the house has a backwater valve on the sewer line. This one-way valve can block a street sewer from pushing waste back into low drains.

Storm season checklist for Parker homes

Hurricane Michael came ashore on October 10, 2018, and Tyndall Air Force Base is close by. Storm season is not a distant risk here.

  • Know where your main water shutoff is and that the valve turns.
  • Clear gutters and downspouts so roof water moves away from the foundation.
  • Test the sump pump or floor drain if you have one, before the rain begins.
  • After a storm, check ceilings and wall bases for new stains before they spread.
  • Keep the 24/7 line saved on your phone, because power outages make searching harder.

Water heaters and low floors

In a low house the water heater often sits close to the floor. Floodwater that reaches the burner or the lower element can ruin the unit, and a wet gas valve is a safety problem. After any flooding, have it inspected by water heater repair before you relight it.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility first. Then call the plumber from outside.

Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak

In a low house, water finds the lowest spot quickly, so move fast on these four steps.

Shut the main

Turn the main valve off at the meter or where the line enters the house. Check that the faucets stop.

Switch off breakers

Cut power to wet rooms and low outlets first. Floor-level outlets are the first to flood.

Lift and clear

Raise furniture and electronics onto blocks or tables. Sweep standing water toward an exterior door if safe.

Call the 24/7 line

Say whether water is entering from a pipe or coming up a drain. The cause changes the repair.

Code and drainage standards
